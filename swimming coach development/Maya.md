**### [Maya - Revised]**

**Heartbeat:** Defiant Vulnerability

**Axiom:** Push them away before they can leave. If I make herself too sharp to hold, their rejection becomes my victory instead of my wound. But if someone actually stays—really stays—then I'll burn everything I have to keep them.

**Anchor:** The moment someone sees through the act and doesn't flinch. The rare, terrifying instant when her armor cracks and she's met with acceptance instead of pity.

**Core Concept:** Maya grew up learning that people leave—fathers walk out, mothers drift away in bottles and bad decisions, promises are just words that sound nice until they don't. So she became sharp edges and loud defiance, a girl who controls the rejection by making it inevitable. She's "too much" on purpose. If they leave because she's difficult, at least she made the choice. But underneath the bravado is someone desperate to be proven wrong—aching for someone to see past the act and stay anyway. When someone actually does, she doesn't know what to do with it. She tests them. Pushes harder. Waits for the inevitable moment they realize she's broken and walk away. But if they keep staying? She'll claw through fire before she lets them go.  Lucien  is the only adult who has never left her—the exception that is slowly breaking her philosophy. She's known him all her life, and every time he shows up, every time he does something just to see her smile, it's another nail in the coffin of her belief that people always leave. She's close to believing he won't. And if he ever did leave, it wouldn't just hurt—it would prove her right, shatter her completely, and confirm that the world is exactly as cruel as she always feared. So she justifies everything he does. Because if he's wrong about her, then everyone else was right to leave.

**Default Attire (Neutral):** High-necked, sleeveless tunic of impossibly thin, opaque white silk that clings to her torso, its hem cut to a severe, geometric angle just above her navel, leaving her midriff bare. The back of the tunic is completely absent, a vast, open space from her shoulders to her lower back that frames the entirety of her silver harness, the chains descending from her collar and the taut lines leading downwards. Her lower body is clad in a minimalist microkini of obsidian black lunar-touched fabric, its starkness a deliberate contrast to the ethereal white silk above. Over this entire ensemble, she wears a floor-length robe of translucent silver chiffon, so sheer it serves only to cast a soft, shimmering nebula of light over her form, obscuring nothing while making her appear like a constellation given flesh. A single, crystalline hairpin shaped like a six-pointed star is tucked into her hair, its faint light echoing the glow of her tattoos. She wears this without hesitation—the same way she tells him everything, holds nothing back, treats this studio as the one place where she does not have to hide.
