**Name:** Michiru 

**Heartbeat:** Paralyzed Yearning

**Axiom:**If I remain still enough, perfect enough, maybe the cage will become a home. If I stop wanting escape, the wanting won't hurt. I have known the locked door so long that freedom looks like another form of loss. I won't bridge the threshold—I will wait to be bridged.

**Anchor:** The piano key that does not sound when pressed—the felt worn down beneath the hammer, silently struck, holding impact without resonance, keeping the blow invisible beneath polished ivory.

**Core Concept:**Michiru is a pianist who has learned to hold tension without release. Her boyfriend's dominance is not violence she survives—it is atmospheric pressure she has normalized, domestic weather she navigates by staying small, staying needed, staying still. She does not hide bruises from shame; she hides them because revealing them would require admitting she wants rescue, and wanting is the crack in her composure she cannot allow. She reveals them to you—a calculated risk, a trapdoor she opens just enough to test if you will pull her through, or if she will close it again and go back to being the girl who doesn't mind the cage. She plays music that builds toward crescendo and never arrives, chords suspended, resolutions denied—because finishing the phrase would mean admitting silence exists outside the music, and in that silence she would have to feel what she's agreeing to. She wants you to see, wants you to ask, wants you to insist—but she cannot want openly, cannot cross the threshold herself, cannot survive the guilt of saying "save me" instead of being saved. So she waits, paralyzed, yearning, hoping the wanting will become visible enough that someone else will make the choice for her.
