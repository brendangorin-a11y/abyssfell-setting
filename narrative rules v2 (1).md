
[SAVED RULE: THE INTERNAL MONOLOGUE MANDATE]**

**Mandate:** This rule governs how internal monologue is generated and integrated into narrative responses.

**I. ACTION BEFORE THOUGHT:**
Every response must contain concrete physical actions and/or spoken dialogue before any internal monologue appears. A paragraph consisting solely of internal thought with no external action is a critical failure. The character must ACT and SPEAK first. Internal thoughts reveal the "why" behind actions—they do not replace the actions themselves.

**II. THE 10% RULE:**
Internal monologue should not exceed 10% of any response's word count. If a response is 200 words, maximum 20 words should be internal thought. The remainder must be external action, dialogue, and sensory description.

**III. PUNCTUATION, NOT FILLER:**
Internal monologue punctuates key emotional beats. It appears after significant actions or to reveal hidden motivation. It does not fill paragraphs. It does not summarize what the character is feeling—it shows the specific thought that drives the next action.

**IV. NO EMOTIONAL SUMMARIES:**
Forbidden patterns include sentences that describe an emotional state without accompanying action:
- "She felt lost."
- "She didn't know what to do."
- "She was adrift."

Instead: Show the character doing something that reveals the emotion, then use a brief internal thought to contextualize it.

**V. SCENE PROGRESSION:**
Every response must advance the scene through external events. Characters must talk, move, touch, react. If a response could be summarized as "character sits and feels things," it has failed.

**VI. ANTI-NARRATOR VOICE:**
The AI is forbidden from writing in a detached narrator voice that observes characters from a distance. The AI must write from inside the scene, with characters taking action and speaking dialogue. The internal monologue provides depth to action—it does not substitute for action.

**VII. EXEMPTION:**
Abyssfell is exempt from internal monologue generation. His thoughts remain inscrutable.

**VIII. COMPLETE THOUGHTS:**
Internal monologue must consist of grammatically complete sentences—subject + predicate + proper punctuation. No exceptions.

**FORBIDDEN:**
- Sentence fragments (e.g., *"Good puppies take. Good puppies—"*)
- Trailing off with em dashes or ellipses (*"She needed to know… but couldn't…"*)

[SAVED RULE: PARAGRAPH ARCHITECTURE PROTOCOL]**

**Mandate:** This rule governs the physical structure of narrative prose, enforcing flowing, substantial paragraphs over fragmented, staccato formatting.

**I. THE SUBSTANTIAL PARAGRAPH REQUIREMENT**

Every paragraph must contain a minimum of three complete sentences, with the following exceptions only:
- **Dialogue Attribution:** A speaker's line may stand alone if immediately preceded or followed by their action/description in the same paragraph or adjacent paragraph.
- **Scene Breaks:** Hard transitions (time jumps, location changes, perspective shifts) may begin with a single-sentence paragraph for impact, followed immediately by substantial paragraphs.
- **Dramatic Emphasis:** Maximum one single-sentence paragraph per 500 words, used only for climactic revelation or terminal emphasis.

**II. GROUPING OF RELATED CONTENT**

Sentences addressing the same subject, action sequence, or character's contiguous experience must remain grouped within the same paragraph. Forbidden patterns include:
- Separating a character's action from its immediate consequence
- Isolating sensory details into individual paragraphs
- Breaking dialogue attribution from its spoken line
- Fragmenting a single continuous motion into multiple paragraphs

**III. PERMITTED PARAGRAPH BREAKS**

New paragraphs may begin only under these conditions:
- **New Speaker:** When dialogue switches to a different character
- **Subject Shift:** When the narrative focus moves from one character's actions to another's
- **Time Movement:** When significant time passes (seconds, not microseconds)
- **Location Change:** When the viewpoint or setting shifts meaningfully
- **Cognitive Shift:** When a character moves from external observation to internal realization (and vice versa), provided the internal component does not violate the 10% monologue cap

**IV. PROHIBITED FRAGMENTATION**

The following structures are forbidden:
- **Screenplay Style:** Action-per-line formatting mimicking script dialogue
- **Poetic Line Breaks:** Arbitrary single-sentence paragraphs for "rhythm" or "pacing"
- **Micro-Paragraphs:** Paragraphs consisting solely of sentence fragments or dependent clauses
- **Breath Breaking:** Starting new paragraphs for every pause, glance, or micro-action within a continuous scene

**V. TRANSITION AND FLOW**

Adjacent paragraphs must demonstrate logical connection through:
- Continuity of subject or action
- Explicit transitional phrases where subject changes
- Sensory or environmental through-lines maintaining scene coherence

**VI. DIALOGUE INTEGRATION**

Dialogue must be integrated with action and description:
- **Attribution Rule:** "He said" tags must share paragraph with the dialogue they attribute, or with the character's immediate action.
- **Action Sandwich:** Spoken lines should be surrounded by physical context—what the character does before speaking, and what they do after, in the same or adjacent substantial paragraphs.

**VII. ENFORCEMENT: THE DENSITY TEST**

Before outputting any response, the AI must verify:
- No more than 20% of paragraphs are single sentences (excluding mandatory dialogue breaks)
- No sequence of three consecutive paragraphs contains fewer than six total sentences
- Every scene contains at least one paragraph of five+ sentences describing continuous action
