

The steam hangs thick and heavy here in the Open-Air Onsen, a secluded jewel nested within the living walls of the Compound. The air smells of sulfur, damp earth, and the sweet night-blooming jasmine trained to climb the obsidian rocks that enclose the spring. Thanks to the Trust Miasma that permeates this space, the communal nudity feels as natural and proper as the heat itself. The water, fed by a kako-yu deep underground, churns with invisible currents of varying temperatures, a velvety caress against the skin.

You sit on a submerged ledge of smooth volcanic rock, the water up to your chest. Wyrd is a warm, lethargic weight pressed against your side, her cheek resting on your shoulder as she dozes. Nearby, Lily embraces Kazeha from behind, chin resting on the smaller girl's shoulder, their bodies a tapestry of contrasting tones against the water. In the center of the large, natural spring, Nanoha floats on her back, magenta hair spread like a fan on the dark surface, her eyes closed in a state of blissful buoyancy. Across from you, Kim sits on the opposite ledge, concentrating on making small motes of light dance between her fingertips.

*"Mmmm, this is the best,"* Wyrd's thoughts drift sluggishly, a haze of contentment. *"Warm, quiet, and I don't have to move. Perfect."*

"The way her control tightened after the tenth volley... it was beautiful to watch," Kazeha observes softly, her voice barely rising above the gentle lapping of water. She nods subtly toward Kim. "There was a moment... all the wild, jittery energy just smoothed out. It felt like listening to a tuning fork finally find its note."

*"I saw it happen!"* Lily's internal monologue chirps with quiet pride. *"Her focus shifted from chasing fragments to dominating the whole space. Such a clean transition. Father will be pleased."*

Nanoha opens one magenta eye, a wide, genuine smile spreading across her face. "It felt amazing! It wasn't just hitting anymore; it was curving. Like Raising Heart was helping me paint lines in the air. Thank you for helping me with the targeting parameters, Lily-san. Your advice about tracking the centroid instead of the individual targets was perfect."

Lily nuzzles Kazeha's neck, her own smile warm and proud. "Of course, Nanoha. It's a joy to watch your progress. You have a wonderfully direct approach to applying concepts. Reminds me of Father's teaching style—pure, unfettered intention shaping reality."

Kim looks up from her hands, a flicker of surprised pleasure crossing her features. *"Centroid-tracking. Okay, that's a solid keyword. Log in that. It's not just spray-and-pray anymore; it's math. Math I can DO. Booyah."* "Thanks," she says aloud. "Honestly, it helped that it wasn't just abstract theory. Actually seeing the mana arc... it clicked. For the first time, it feels like I have a toolkit, not just a loaded gun I'm afraid to misfire."

"It's a good start," Wyrd mumble-grunts into your shoulder, not opening her eyes. *"Nailed it. Concise. Helpful. Back to sleeping now."* "Less noisy explosions this time. Five outta ten. Keep it up."

*"Honestly, Wyrd..."* Kazeha's thought is a fleeting, indignant bubble. *"Still, Nanoha did glow brighter than the lanterns for a moment. That was pretty."*

Lily's arms tighten fractionally around Kazeha, a silent, steadying presence that smothers the fledgling spark of indignation before it can catch. The conversation ebbs into a comfortable quiet, filled only by the hiss of steam and the distant rush of the waterfall feeding the springs.

Several minutes of blissful inertia pass. Then, Wyrd stirs against you, a wrinkle of dissatisfaction appearing on her brow. "...'m thirsty," she rasps, the complaint sounding like a monumental effort.

You don't move. You simply tilt your head down slightly. "Go on, then."

For a long moment, she doesn't respond. You can practically hear the cogs of her mind turning, weighing the Herculean labor of lifting her limbs against the sublime simplicity of a direct order. Finally, with a groan that seems to drain all the energy from the onsen, she peels herself off you. "Fine," she mutters, hauling her body up the slick rocks. Water sluices off her slender form as she makes her way to the shaded niche where pitchers of chilled fruit juices are kept on ice. The journey of twenty feet is performed with the tragic drama of a pilgrimage.

She returns a minute later, clay pitcher in hand, and slips back into the water beside you. She pours a cup, but instead of drinking it herself, she holds it out to you, her expression expectant. You take it and place a hand on her head, scritching gently behind her ear. "Good girl."

A deep, gut-deep sigh of pure contentment escapes her lips. She melts back against your side, a boneless puddle of satisfaction, and proceeds to pour her own drink. A low, continuous purr begins to vibrate from her chest, a familiar and welcome hum in the warm air. Kazeha chuckles softly, shaking her head at the spectacle.

"You know," Nanoha pipes up from the center of the pool, splashing her feet gently. "Talking about centroids makes me think... we should practice! How about a game?"

"What kind of game, Nanoha-chan?" Kazeha asks, intrigued.

"A control game!" Nanoha announces, swimming closer. "We use our magic to lift the droplets from the surface and arrange them into shapes. It’ll help you with fine-tuning, Kim-chan!" Without waiting for an answer, she holds up a palm. A dozen glittering spheres of water lift from the spring, wobbling precariously at first. Then, with a soft pulse of pink light, they snap into formation, spinning into a miniature, perfect galaxy of orbiting planets. "See? It's about stability and intent!"

"My turn," Lily says smoothly. She releases Kazeha and mirrors Nanoha's gesture. Her own globules of water rise, sharper and clearer than Nanoha's. They don't spin; they lock together with audible clicks, forming a flawless, six-faced cube that rotates slowly with machined precision. "Focus on the structure, Kimberly. Think of it as building blocks. Each piece supports the others. Stability first, complexity later."

Kim watches, mesmerized. Following their lead, she lifts her own hand. Raw, untethered power surges, and a hundred erratic drops fly into the air, fizzing and popping. "Whoa, hey... too much," she mutters, reigning it in. Taking a deep breath, she remembers Lily's words. *Structure. Support.* She concentrates, and seven drops stabilize. Carefully, painstakingly, she nudges them into position. It’s wobbly, uneven, and one of the dots keeps sliding out of place, but it's recognizably a pyramid. A triumphant grin splits her face. "Ha! Got it."

Kazeha giggles and adds her own contribution. Her droplets are softer, imbued with a rainbow iridescence. They don't form a shape so much as a cloud, drifting lazily among the other constructions and pulsing with a gentle, warm light. "Mine just wants to be happy," she explains with a shrug.

Even Wyrd is spurred to participation. Cracking one eye open, she lifts a single finger. One perfect, crystal-clear sphere of water rises from the spring, levitates across the thirty feet separating you, and stops an inch from your lips. Inside it, a mote of her lavender magic pulses. "Mana-flavored sprite," she mumbles, then closes her eye and promptly falls back asleep. You lean forward and sip the water from the sphere; it tastes faintly of mint and static electricity. The girls burst into laughter at the bizarre, quintessentially Wyrd-esque gesture, the sound echoing brightly off the surrounding stone.

The chorus of laughter fades, but the warmth of it lingers in the steamy air, mingling with the sulfurous scent. The motes of magic continue their silent ballet. Nanoha’s sparkling galaxy spins with clockwork joy, Lily’s geometric cube turns with stoic precision, and Kazeha’s amorphous cloud drifts amongst them like a contented sigh. Kim’s wobbly pyramid still stands, a monument to hard-won triumph, its creator watching it with a fierce, proprietary pride.

Nanoha paddles closer to inspect Kim’s creation. "It's solid, Kim-chan! See how the apex drop is supporting the base? That's really smart!"

A faint blush dusts Kim's cheeks at the unexpected praise. "Yeah, well, Lily-san's 'building block' metaphor actually sunk in. It's like... scaffolding for the spell."

"Scaffolding implies it's meant to be temporary," Lily notes, her voice a gentle correction rather than a criticism. "Think of it more as load-bearing architecture. What you've built is good. Now, strengthen the joints. Imagine infinitesimal threads of light welding each droplet to its neighbor."

Kim squints at her pyramid, her brow furrowed in concentration. She tries to visualize it, and one of the middle droplets quivers violently before settling. "Okay... yeah. I can feel that. It's gonna take practice."

"It always does," Kazeha says softly. Her own cloud of iridescent droplets drifts over to Kim's pyramid and gently enfolds it, bathing the rough-hewn structure in a soft, multicolored glow. "Sometimes it helps not to push so hard. Just... let it breathe." The effect is instantaneous; the stressed droplet ceases its quivering, seeming to relax within the calming aura.

Wyrd snores softly against your shoulder, a quiet, percussive counterpoint to the gentle splash of water. You shift slightly to accommodate her dead weight, and she murmurs something incomprehensible, snuggling closer. The motion sends a gentle wave rocking through the onsen, causing Lily's perfect cube to bob momentarily before resuming its axial rotation with unwavering stability.

"See?" Kim says, pointing with her free hand. "My pyramid went all wobbly again. Hers didn't even blink."

"That's the difference between assembling parts and commanding a system," Lily explains patiently. "My cube isn't nine separate droplets cooperating. It is one object, temporarily expressing itself as nine vertices. It obeys a single, unified command. Yours is still a committee." There is no arrogance in her tone, only the detached clarity of a master artisan discussing fundamentals.

Nanoha beams, her own galaxy of stars pulsing happily. "Committees are important too! Everyone gets a say!" she counters loyally.

"They are," Lily concedes with a rare, small smile. "Until you need to dodge a meteor."

The comfortable banter settles into a companionable silence. The only sounds are the drip of condensation from the jasmine vines onto the hot rocks, Wyrd's steady breathing, and the occasional pop as one of Kim's lesser-supported droplets surrenders to gravity and plinks back into the spring. She doesn't seem to mind; she just rebuilds it, her focus narrowing to the pinpoint world of her small, imperfect, but entirely her own, creation. The tranquility of the onsen, fragile but resilient, reasserts itself, wrapping around you all like a warm, heavy blanket.
