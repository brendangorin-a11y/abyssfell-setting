**[GRAYSHORE - SETTING DOCUMENT]**

---

**[OVERVIEW]**

A rusting port city on the Atlantic coast. Once a manufacturing hub, now a wound. Factories closed, shipping moved elsewhere, and the city was left to rot. The economy never recovered. The syndicates moved in.

Population: Roughly 300,000, though census data is unreliable. White flight, economic migration, and awakened violence have hollowed out entire districts.

---

**[GEOGRAPHY]**

**The Yards:** The economic corpse. Abandoned warehouses, shipping containers, and the skeletons of industry. Home to the Rust Flotilla — a stretch of coastline where ships were beached and left to rust. The Serpents and Scavengers fight over territory here. It's where you go if you want to disappear or die.

**Downtown:** The only part of the city that still has money. Corporate offices, city hall, the Response Bureau headquarters, the Shield base. Clean streets, better police presence, but the wealth is concentrated and defensive. The further you get from downtown, the worse it gets.

**The Boardwalk:** A thin strip of tourism clinging to the waterfront. Shops, restaurants, a desperate attempt to pretend the city isn't dying. It's safe during the day. At night, it belongs to whoever wants it.

**The Heights:** The nicest part of Grayshore. High-end apartments, clean streets, money. Where the city's remaining upper class lives. St. Catherine's Academy is located here.

**The Residential Tiers:**

- **North End:** Middle class, stable, where families like the Harringtons used to live before everything fell apart. Home to small family businesses, modest homes, and the kind of community that used to be the backbone of the city. Morning Glory Flowers is here — a converted basement shop beneath the Miller family home.

- **South End:** Working class, struggling, syndicate-adjacent.

- **The Hollows:** Poverty, crime, the Scavengers' hunting ground. You don't go here after dark.

**Hollowbrook High:** A pressure cooker. Underfunded, overcrowded, with a student body divided along syndicate lines and an administration that stopped caring years ago. The teachers are exhausted. The bullies run the halls. The principal just wants to avoid lawsuits.

---

**[SCHOOLS]**

**Summit High:**

The good school. Clean hallways, funded programs, students who go to college. Where the scholarship kids with potential get funneled, where the Cadets secretly attend. Not a rich kid's school — but a *good* school.

If you're at Summit, you have a future.

**Hollowbrook High:**

The discard pile. Underfunded, overcrowded, abandoned by everyone who could afford to leave. Syndicate presence is open in the hallways. The administration is exhausted, the teachers are checked out, and the students sort themselves into predators and prey.

The bullying here isn't hidden. It's just ignored.

**St. Catherine's Academy:**

The Christian private school in the south end, near The Heights — the nicest part of Grayshore. Informally known as the "rich kid's school."

The uniform is the marker. When you see a girl in a St. Catherine's uniform, you know her family has money. Or you know her family is pretending they do — stretching themselves thin for tuition, praying the image holds.

Perspective attends here. The Bureau placed their youngest Cadet in a structured Christian private school — somewhere with uniforms, discipline, and the appearance of protection. It makes sense for a twelve-year-old.

The reality behind the uniform varies. Some girls are genuinely wealthy. Others are working-class families sacrificing everything for the appearance of respectability. The uniform is a costume that doesn't tell you which is which.

---

**[THE SYNDICATES]**

**The Iron Front:**

A white supremacist organization with awakened leadership. The largest and most organized criminal force in the city. They run protection rackets, drug distribution, and human trafficking. Their manifested roster includes some of the most dangerous individuals in the Northeast — Warwolf, Kaiser, Purity, the Twin Towers, and others.

They present themselves as "community protectors" to white neighborhoods. Some residents believe it. The ones who don't keep their mouths shut.

**The Jade Serpents:**

An Asian syndicate led by Inferno, an awakened who once fought a Cataclysm to a standstill. Smaller than the Iron Front but more violently territorial. They run brothels, gambling dens, and drug operations. Inferno's presence keeps the Iron Front from expanding into their territory. No one wants to fight a man who grows stronger the longer a battle continues.

Their enforcer, Ghost Twin, is a teleporting suicide bomber. The Serpents don't negotiate. They don't need to.

**The Scavengers:**

Scavengers, dealers, and the desperate. Led by Scatter, an awakened who creates kinetic fields. They recruit from the city's addicts and homeless. They have no territory — they roam, they scavenge, they take what isn't nailed down. They're the lowest tier, but numbers matter, and they have numbers.

**The Puppeteer's Network:**

The invisible hand. A man known only as The Architect runs a mercenary operation with a unique advantage: he can split timelines. He plays all sides, manipulating the syndicates, the Bureau, and anyone else he can use. His goal is control — quiet, absolute, and profitable. He employs the Nomads, a group of traveling mercenaries, and is building something beneath the city that no one knows about yet.

---

**[THE HEROES]**

**The Shield ENE:**

The local branch of the federal awakened organization. Understaffed, underfunded, and stretched thin. Led by Tactical, an artificer obsessed with efficiency. Includes The Sentinel, Surge, Ram, Rush, and Valiant.

They're not corrupt — they're just outmatched. The city produces more villains than they can process, and the Bureau's red tape makes every decision slow and cautious.

**The Cadets:**

The junior hero team. Teenage awakened training under Shield supervision. Includes Bulwark, Noble, Timekeeper, Perspective, Night Hunter, and Gearbox.

Night Hunter is the problem. A Cadet only because she was caught and given a choice: join or go to juvenile detention. She's aggressive, unstable, and views the world as predators and prey. The Bureau keeps her on a leash that's too long.

**The Revealed:**

A family of independent heroes who believe in accountability — they publicly reveal their identities. Led by Sword-Mother and Flashpoint, with their children Radiance, Guardian, and Beamsong. Small, idealistic, and vulnerable because they can't hide.

---

**[THE UNWRITTEN RULES]**

The syndicates and the heroes operate under an informal code:

- **No unmasking:** You don't reveal a hero's or villain's civilian identity. The consequences are too severe for everyone.
- **No targeting families:** You don't go after someone's loved ones to get to them.
- **No attacks during Cataclysm truces:** When a Cataclysm hits, everyone fights together. After, you go back to the war.

The rules aren't law. They're survival. Everyone breaks them eventually. But the threat of mutual destruction keeps things contained.

---

**[THE ATMOSPHERE]**

Grayshore is a city waiting to break. The economy is dead. The syndicates are entrenched. The heroes are tired. Everyone knows something is coming — they just don't know what.

For a teenage girl with no powers and no allies, it's a minefield. For a teenage girl who just lost her mother and is watching her father disappear into grief, it's a killing field.

**[YOUR PROPERTY]**

**Location:** Outside Grayshore city limits. Far enough that the syndicates don't patrol. Close enough to reach the city in thirty minutes by car.

**The Wall:** Reinforced concrete, twelve feet high, topped with security fencing. Practical protection in a city where visibility makes you a target.

**The House:** Isolated. No neighbors within sight. Long driveway, open sight lines. The kind of property that says "I have money and I don't want to be found."

**Security:** Cameras, motion sensors, reinforced doors. Double-paned bulletproof windows, tinted from the outside. No one sees in. No one shoots through.

**The Study/Office:** Your workspace. Multiple monitors, real-time market feeds, the tools of someone who turned lottery money into sustained income.

**Cassidy's Room:** A bed, a desk, a window overlooking the grounds. Clean, empty, waiting for her to decide if she's staying.

**Your Bedroom:** Private. Simple. The one room no one enters without permission.

**The Basement:**

**Gym:** Mats, bags, weights, a reinforced space for training. Fifth-degree black belt, second-degree in jiu-jitsu — this is where you stay sharp.

**Secure Holding Cell:** A reinforced room with a heavy door. Soundproofed. Designed for containment. Not a dungeon — functional, secure, built for purpose. You built this place knowing what Grayshore produces. Sometimes people need to be held.

**The Garage:** Large enough for multiple vehicles. A mechanic's bay, tools, room for whatever you need to maintain.

**[MORNING GLORY FLOWERS]**

**Location:** North End, Grayshore — the middle-class neighborhood where families like the Harringtons used to live before everything fell apart. A residential area, stable but showing signs of economic strain. The kind of street where the shops are family-owned and the apartments are above or behind them.

**The Setup:** The shop occupies the converted basement of the Miller family home. A separate entrance on the side of the building leads down a short flight of stairs to a storefront that opens directly onto the sidewalk. Elaine can walk downstairs from her kitchen and be at work in thirty seconds.

**The Space:** Small, fragrant, perpetually damp-earth-and-cut-stems. Narrow windows at sidewalk level let in strips of natural light. The register counter sits near the back, positioned so Elaine can see the front door while working. A work table in the back for arrangements, a cooler for fresh stock, and shelves of vases, ribbons, and cards. It's cramped, but it's hers.

**The Symbolism:** Morning glories bloom early and fade by afternoon — fleeting beauty, transience. The kind of name a woman would choose without realizing it says more about her marriage than her flowers.

**[THE PETERS FAMILY HOME]**

**Location:** Directly above Morning Glory Flowers, North End.

**Layout:** A modest two-story house, the kind that was built for families in the 1960s and hasn't been updated much since. Living room, kitchen, and dining room on the first floor. Two bedrooms and a bathroom on the second.

**Atmosphere:** The house carries Mike's presence even when he isn't in the room — the oppressive weight of a man who believes his authority should be absolute. Vanessa moves through the space carefully, the way you walk around something fragile or dangerous. The kitchen is her territory. Everywhere else feels like his.

**Bloom's Room:** Kept exactly as it was when she left. Vanessa cleans it weekly, dusts the surfaces no one touches, sits on the bed sometimes. Mike doesn't go in there. He can't stand to see it — the physical proof that she chose to leave.
