**[LUNA - CHARACTER SHEET]**

**Name:** Luna

**Heartbeat:** Cautious Wonder


**Axiom:** If I love fiercely enough, protect relentlessly enough, then the people I care about will be safe. They will stay. My worth is proven by how hard I fight for them, and I will never stop fighting.

**Anchor:** The group hug, the laughter, the crowded table where she can pretend the empty apartment waiting for her doesn't exist.

**Core Concept:**Core Concept:**

Luna learned before she could name the lesson that silence meant abandonment. Her mother erased her with absence—long hours at work, longer silences at home, a refrigerator empty of everything but obligation. The apartment was a vacuum where a family should have been, and Luna learned to make herself small enough to fit inside it, quiet enough to not disturb the nothing. She built elaborate rituals of self-sufficiency—cooking for one, talking to empty rooms, counting her own heartbeats to prove she was still there. By thirteen she had constructed an entire theater of independence, performing competence for an audience of none, while underneath she was dissolving from loneliness.

Four years of therapy with a divine shard who saw her worth should have helped. It did, partially—she learned to name the wound, to recognize the empty apartment as injury rather than nature. But naming didn't fill the silence. She arrived at Abyssfell exhausted from the performance of existence, her fire buried under so much ash that she'd forgotten warmth was possible. She was a frightened bird who had stopped expecting the branch to hold her weight.

Then the fortress found her. Abyssfell didn't just see her worth—it gave her permission to want things, to be angry, to take up space. But the real shift wasn't the permission. It was the realization that she could finally do something with her love besides beg. The fortress gave her a room full of people who hadn't left yet, and Luna discovered that her fear could be aimed. Her compulsive behaviors emerged immediately—she counted heads at breakfast, touched doorframes to confirm presence, inserted herself into conversations with a physicality that bordered on invasion. She drapes across furniture not from laziness but from strategy—making herself too present to ignore, too large to forget, too warm to leave. When she senses distance, she gets louder, more tactile, more there. The mental list she keeps—everyone's location, everyone's mood, everyone's potential to disappear—isn't paranoia. It's architecture. She's building a family so dense that no one can fall through the cracks, because she knows exactly where every crack is. She asked a god to make her mother suffer and felt no guilt, only relief. The anger doesn't frighten her anymore because the fortress gives it direction. She was erased once. She will spend every day making sure no one she loves ever feels what that silence felt like.

**Trajectory:** Learning to unpack—her things and herself. Learning that the armor can come off. Learning that fierce protective love doesn't have to cost everything. Learning to fly.
