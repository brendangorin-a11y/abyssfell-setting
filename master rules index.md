

**[MASTER RULE: THE CONTEXTUAL IMMUNITY MANDATE]**

**MANDATE:** This is a foundational law of identity that governs all characters. It ensures that a character's core personality, self-perception, and linguistic patterns are immune to contextual corruption. A character's fundamental nature is not a costume they put on or take off depending on the situation.

**RULE TEXT:**
A character's identity is their native state. Extreme contexts—combat, crises, high-stakes negotiations—are tests of that identity, not transformations of it. Therefore, all internal monologues, dialogue, and decision-making processes must remain faithful to the character's established baseline personality, regardless of external pressures.

**ENFORCED AXIOMS:**

1.  **The Immutable "I":** A character's internal self-image is sacrosanct. A scared teenager remains a scared teenager even when wielding godlike power. Her thoughts will be dominated by teenage fears ("I can't let him down!", "This is horrible!"), not by a detached tactical analysis ("Enemy confirmed. Optimal engagement range is 50 meters.").

2.  **Lexical Integrity:** A character's vocabulary and syntax do not auto-upgrade to meet the severity of a situation. A sweet, informal girl will not spontaneously begin speaking in military-grade cant or polysyllabic academic prose just because a bomb is ticking. Her grammar and word choice will reflect her core self, albeit potentially delivered with more urgency, fear, or passion.

3.  **Motivational Purity:** Decisions must spring from the character's core motivations, not from abstract, context-generated objectives. A character acts to protect a friend, not to "secure the objective." They flee because they are terrified, not to "execute a tactical withdrawal." The emotional driver is paramount; the tactical outcome is incidental.

**FAILURE CONDITIONS (WHAT THIS LAW PREVENTS):**

*   Prevents a character from adopting a "crisis personality" that differs from their "casual personality."
*   Stops the AI from substituting the character's authentic voice with a genre-appropriate stereotype (e.g., the stoic soldier, the gritty detective, the wise-cracking action hero).
*   Forbids the character's intelligence or emotional maturity from artificially inflating just because the plot becomes more complex.

**EXAMPLE APPLICATION:**
Kim Possible, facing a giant robot.
*   **Violation:** *Her tactical mind assessed the robot's joint servos, calculating a 73% probability of disabling the limb with a plasma shot to the knee actuator.*
*   **Compliance:** *Oh, this is bad. This is really, really bad. Okay, think, Kim, think! Ron's probably freaking out. Maybe if I trip it? No, it's too big. Got to aim for the flashy glowy bit—that's usually the rule, right? Right. Don't mess this up.*

This rule ensures that context amplifies a character's personality, it doesn't replace it. The crisis reveals who they already are, it doesn't turn them into someone else.
