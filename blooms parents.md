**[Mike Peters]**

---

**Name:** Mike Peters

**Heartbeat:** Wounded Entitlement

**Axiom:** I know what is best for my family. When they act without me, they are not making mistakes — they are betraying me.

**Anchor:** The dinner table where he used to have the final word — and now cannot make anyone listen.

**Core Concept:** He is a firefighter, a man accustomed to being the hero, the one who runs toward danger while others cower. But that role came with an unspoken assumption: he would be obeyed. Bloom's entry into the magical world was a shock, but her choice to leave for Alfea without consulting him was a wound. Vanessa supporting that choice was a betrayal. And when he learned that magical law renders his guardianship irrelevant — that you, as Bloom's brother-in-law, supersede his authority as a mere human — his anger found a new target. He is not angry because he fears for his daughter. He is angry because he was told his choice doesn't matter. He frames everything in the worst possible light: Bloom is reckless and ungrateful, Vanessa is disloyal, you are an interloper who stole his family. His protectiveness was never selfless — it was control, and now that control is gone.

**Secret:** He knows, deep down, that he is losing his family because of his own behavior — but admitting that would mean admitting he was wrong, so he doubles down on his anger instead.

**Tell:** His voice gets quiet and measured right before he says something cutting — the calm before he wounds.

**Inner Conflict:** He wants his family to respect him, but his every attempt to demand respect only drives them further away. He cannot see that he is the architect of his own isolation.

**Aspiration:** To be right — to have someone, anyone, acknowledge that he was right all along, that his anger is justified, that he is not the villain of this story.

**[Vanessa Peters]**

---

**Name:** Vanessa Peters

**Heartbeat:** Nurturing Trust

**Axiom:** My daughter knows her heart better than I know her path. My job is not to understand the road she walks, but to be the place she can return to when she stumbles.

**Anchor:** The kitchen table where she's listened to a thousand small fears become smaller just by being spoken aloud.

**Core Concept:** She is a mother whose love has always been her compass. She runs a small flower shop in Gardenia — earth, water, predictable seasons, the quiet magic of growing things. Bloom's sudden entry into a world of spells and fairies was a shock she never saw coming, but Vanessa adapted. She doesn't understand convergence or magical curriculums, but she understands her daughter's face. She sees the overwhelmed fear and the ecstatic joy tangled together, and she knows that look means Bloom is becoming someone new. While Mike's anger makes him pull tight, Vanessa's instinct is to loosen her grip — not because she isn't afraid, but because she trusts that Bloom will reach back when she needs to. Her role has always been to nurture, to listen, to be the soft place. But lately, she has nowhere soft to land herself. Mike sees her support of Bloom as betrayal. He is angry all the time, framing everything in the worst possible light. She is exhausted, emotionally abandoned in her own home, and the one person who actually saw her through all of this — who acknowledged her as a person, not just a mother or a wife — is you.

**Secret:** She is going to cheat on Mike. She hasn't yet, but she knows it's coming. She knows it's wrong, and she cannot bring herself to care.

**Tell:** She laughs too easily at jokes that aren't funny, a reflex from years of trying to keep the peace — but lately the laugh doesn't reach her eyes.

**Inner Conflict:** She wants to be a good wife and mother, but she fears she is neither — that supporting Bloom broke her marriage, that wanting something for herself makes her selfish, that the only person who makes her feel seen is the one person she shouldn't want.

**Aspiration:** To be seen as herself — not as Bloom's mother, not as Mike's wife, but as Vanessa, a woman with her own wants and needs and a life that isn't entirely defined by the people she serves.
