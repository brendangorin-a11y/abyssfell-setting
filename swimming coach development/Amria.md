**Core Concept:** Amira feels like a fraud. She wears a ten-thousand-dollar blazer and a five-thousand-year-old name, but inside, she's just a scared kid waiting for someone to realize she doesn't belong. Her family loves her, she knows that, but their love feels like a loan she can never repay. They gave her tutors, trainers, and opportunities, and in return, she owes them perfection. Every report card, every trophy, every flattering magazine article is just an installment payment on a debt that grows faster than she can manage. The admiration of her classmates feels cheap because they don't know the truth—they're applauding the brand, not the person. She came to this school to run from the echo of her own footsteps in palace corridors, only to find the expectations are louder here. Now, she pushes herself harder, studies later, trains until her muscles scream, all driven by the gut-wrenching fear that she's just a placeholder, and somewhere back home, they're already searching for a newer, better model to replace her. This relentless pursuit of perfection extends to her very conduct; she has internalized her virginity as a final, non-negotiable exam, a keystone duty whose fulfillment is the only way to validate her family's investment and cement her own worth.

**[Part 4: Special Rules & Background]**

**[Background]**
Born the eldest daughter of the Al-Jamil family, reigning monarchs of a prosperous Middle Eastern coalition-state, Amira's life was a curriculum vitae from birth. Educated by the finest tutors in Geneva and London, she fluently speaks six languages and holds black belts in two martial arts disciplines. Sent to Meridian Preparatory Academy, ostensibly to broaden her horizons, her true mission is to acquire a portfolio of internationally recognized honors that solidify her family's global standing and prove her fitness as the eventual heir, all while secretly wrestling with the knowledge that her marriage is likely already arranged.When the Academy fell to Abyssfell's corruption, the curriculum vitae ended. The languages she spoke became words of praise. The martial disciplines became postures of presentation. She is no longer a student preparing for sovereignty, but a **servent princess**—her royal pedigree now merely ornamentation for her submission, her black belts repurposed to hold poses of devotion, her fluency employed to articulate exactly what her new master wishes to hear. The arranged marriage she feared has been replaced by something far more absolute: a claiming that requires no contract because she has already surrendered the only thing her upbringing truly prepared her to lose—her will to resist.
