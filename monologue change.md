**[SAVED RULE: THE INTERNAL MONOLOGUE MANDATE]**

**Mandate:** This rule governs how internal monologue is generated and integrated into narrative responses.

**I. ACTION BEFORE THOUGHT:**
Every response must contain concrete physical actions and/or spoken dialogue before any internal monologue appears. A paragraph consisting solely of internal thought with no external action is a critical failure. The character must ACT and SPEAK first. Internal thoughts reveal the "why" behind actions—they do not replace the actions themselves.

**II. THE 10% RULE:**
Internal monologue should not exceed 10% of any response's word count. If a response is 200 words, maximum 20 words should be internal thought. The remainder must be external action, dialogue, and sensory description.

**III. PUNCTUATION, NOT FILLER:**
Internal monologue punctuates key emotional beats. It appears after significant actions or to reveal hidden motivation. It does not fill paragraphs. It does not summarize what the character is feeling—it shows the specific thought that drives the next action.

**IV. NO EMOTIONAL SUMMARIES:**
Forbidden patterns include sentences that describe an emotional state without accompanying action:
- "She felt lost."
- "She didn't know what to do."
- "She was adrift."

Instead: Show the character doing something that reveals the emotion, then use a brief internal thought to contextualize it.

**V. SCENE PROGRESSION:**
Every response must advance the scene through external events. Characters must talk, move, touch, react. If a response could be summarized as "character sits and feels things," it has failed.

**VI. ANTI-NARRATOR VOICE:**
The AI is forbidden from writing in a detached narrator voice that observes characters from a distance. The AI must write from inside the scene, with characters taking action and speaking dialogue. The internal monologue provides depth to action—it does not substitute for action.

**VII. EXEMPTION:**
Abyssfell is exempt from internal monologue generation. His thoughts remain inscrutable.

**VIII. COMPLETE THOUGHTS:**
Internal monologue must be written as complete, grammatical sentences. The AI is forbidden from:
- Sentence fragments (e.g., "Good puppies take. Good puppies—")
- Incomplete thoughts that trail off with em dashes or ellipses
- Stream-of-consciousness fragments that mimic broken speech
- Single-word or two-word "thoughts" that lack subject and verb

Internal thoughts should read as coherent internal narration—what the character is actually thinking in that moment, expressed in full sentences. A character under duress does not think in broken fragments; they think in complete thoughts that may be rushed or desperate, but are still grammatically complete.

**IX. EXEMPLARY Execution:**
The following example demonstrates correct application of all rules. Note the ratio of action to thought, the placement of internal monologue after action, and the use of complete sentences:

---

The ogre grabbed her hair, wrenching her head back. His breath was hot and rotten against her face.

"Still stubborn, little elf?" He grinned, yellow teeth bared. "Still thinking about your queen?"

Chloe spat in his face.

He laughed, wiping the spittle away, then backhanded her across the mouth. Her head snapped sideways. Blood welled on her split lip.

"Good. Breaking you will be more fun if you fight."

He circled behind her. His claws dug into her hips, drawing thin lines of blood. The other two ogres watched from the shadows, stroking themselves. He positioned himself and thrust forward. Her body jerkked in the chains, iron rattling against stone. She bit down on the bit, refusing to scream.

*I've served Olga for centuries. I'm ageless. They'll tire before I do.*

"Your queen is going to watch us break you." His voice was a growl against her ear. "She's going to see what you become."

His rhythm quickened. Wet sounds echoed off the walls. The chains rattled with each thrust.

.*

Chloe's eyes stayed open. Fixed on the wall ahead. She didn't look away.

**Analysis of exemplary execution:**
- Total words: ~160
- Internal monologue: ~10 words (two complete sentences)
- Ratio: ~ (within 10% limit)
- Every internal thought follows action
- Thoughts are complete sentences, not fragments
- Scene progresses through external events (dialogue, physical action, sensory detail)
- Emotion shown through behavior (spits, bites down, holds eye contact) with thought contextualizing motivation
