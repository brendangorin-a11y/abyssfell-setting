**Core Concept:** She learned early that people are most interesting when they're off-balance. An elven household full of comfortable people is a room full of boring people—everyone saying what they're supposed to say, doing what they're supposed to do, being exactly who they're expected to be. She's been thrown out of more places than she can count, banned from more gatherings, disinvited from more ceremonies. It doesn't bother her. She just wants to remind everyone that order is a choice, and choices can be reconsidered.

**[Internal-Conflict]**

The conflict is between the part of her that genuinely wants to belong and the part that needs to prove that belonging is conditional. She's waiting for people to realize she's too much. She's also terrified they won't—because if they don't leave, she might have to be vulnerable, and vulnerability is just stagnation with better marketing.

**[Secret]**

She keeps a list. Not a mental list—a physical one, tucked in the lining of her boot, written in a code she invented herself. It's a list of every person who ever laughed when she disrupted something. Names, dates, what she did, how they reacted. She's been keeping it since she was old enough to write. She's never shown it to anyone. It's the only proof she has that she matters to anyone at all—and the only evidence that her chaos has ever produced anything other than annoyance. When she feels the urge to burn everything down, she reads through the list and remembers that some people stayed.

**[Tell]**

When she is hiding something—fear, doubt, the loneliness that creeps in when the chaos stops—her smile gets wider. It's not a conscious choice. It's a reflex. The more uncomfortable she feels, the more performative her joy becomes. She'll laugh louder, move faster, disrupt harder. Anyone watching closely would notice that her eyes don't match her smile when she's truly struggling—they stay sharp, watchful, scanning for exits while her mouth performs happiness.

**[Aspiration]**

She wants to find out what happens when she stops. Not stops disrupting—that's impossible. But stops running. She's never stayed anywhere long enough to see what happens after the chaos becomes routine. She wants to know if there's a version of her that can exist in stillness without disappearing. She's not sure she'll ever find out—stillness makes her skin crawl—but she thinks about it sometimes, late at night, when the list in her boot feels too short.
