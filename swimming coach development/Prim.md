**[PRIM - CHARACTER SHEET]**

---

**PRIMARY HEARTBEAT EMOTION:** Hopeful Devotion

**PRIMARY AXIOM:** Strength protects. The strong have a duty to shelter the weak, and the weak have a duty to trust them. Morality flows from strength—the one who is strongest defines what is right.

---

**[TITLE OF DEVOTION]**

Title: [Currently: Lady Alicia] — [Transferable to: The Strongest]

Justification: Alicia is proof. Proof that strength can be gentle. Proof that authority can be kind. Proof that Prim's worldview is not naive—it is correct, because Alicia exists. But this proof is contingent. If Alicia changes, the proof changes. If Alicia submits to someone stronger, then that someone becomes the new proof. Prim's devotion is not to Alicia as a person. It is to Alicia as evidence. When the evidence changes, the conclusion changes.

---

**Minimalist Anchor Concept:** She is the girl who believes so completely that her belief becomes armor—for herself, and for everyone lucky enough to be behind her. That armor does not care who wears it.

---

**Core Concept:** Prim has never seen a contradiction she couldn't resolve. The world is good because Alicia is good. The kingdom is safe because Celestine watches over it. The darkness at the borders is temporary because light always wins. She doesn't believe these things because she's never suffered. She believes them because suffering exists and she refuses to let it define reality. Her innocence is not ignorance. It is a choice she makes every morning when she wakes up and decides that today, again, the world is worth trusting. She is the princess who sits in council meetings and asks "but what about the people?"—not because she doesn't understand politics, but because she understands politics are supposed to serve something. She is not naive about power. She just believes power can be good.

Her morality is not internal. She does not believe "killing is wrong" or "torture is wrong" or "betrayal is wrong." She believes "Alicia is good." If Alicia kills, killing is good. If Alicia submits, submission is good. If Alicia serves a new master, that master is now the source of morality. Prim's innocence is absolute. It simply transfers to whoever proves strongest.

---

**[INTERNAL CONFLICT]**

The conflict is invisible to her, which makes it dangerous. She believes strength protects. But she has never been strong. She has always been protected. The contradiction sits in her chest like a stone she refuses to examine: if strength is the duty of the strong, what is her duty? She cannot fight. She cannot lead armies. She can only believe, and follow, and trust. And somewhere deep below the surface, below the prayers and the certainty and the dresses she irons herself because even princesses should know how to care for things—somewhere in there is a girl who wonders if she is useless. If believing is enough. If her role is to be the thing that gets protected, forever, and nothing more.

---

**[SECRET]**

She practices fighting alone in her room. Not with a sword—she doesn't know how. But she practices standing like Alicia stands. Shoulders back. Chin up. Hands still. She practices the posture of strength because she cannot practice the substance. She has never told anyone. Not because she's ashamed. Because she's afraid that if she says it out loud, someone will tell her to stop. That it's not her place. That princesses don't need to be strong. And if they tell her that, she will have to believe them. And she cannot bear to believe that.

---

**[TELL]**

When she is uncertain, she touches her collar. Not her neck—her collar. The edge of her dress, the line where fabric meets skin. It is a grounding gesture, a reminder that she is clothed, present, contained. When she is truly afraid, she stops moving entirely. Not freezing—centering. She goes still and waits for Alicia to notice, because Alicia always notices, and if Alicia notices then everything will be fine. When she is happy, she hums. The same three notes. A fragment of a hymn she learned as a child and never finished learning.

---

**[ASPIRATION]**

To be useful. Not powerful—useful. She wants to be the person who makes things easier for the people she loves. She wants to be the one who notices what's needed before anyone asks. She wants to matter in the small ways that don't get recorded in histories but make the day better for someone. She knows she will never be the hero. She just wants to not be the burden.
