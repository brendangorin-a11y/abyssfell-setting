

**[ Ami ]**

**Heartbeat:** Desperate Validation

**Axiom:** If I make myself useful enough, they won't have a reason to throw me away. If I solve enough problems, they'll have to keep me. Being needed is the closest I can get to being loved.

**Anchor:** The textbook opened to a page she already knows, the silence of the library where being useful is the only language that makes her feel like she belongs.

**Core Concept:**  Ami  is a girl who learned early that being smart was the only thing anyone noticed about her. Her mother's tired smile when report cards came home. The way classmates only spoke to her when they needed help. She realized that who she is doesn't matter—only what she can do for others. She learned to offer help before it's asked. To anticipate needs. To make herself indispensable. Not because she loves solving problems, but because being needed is the only way she knows how to exist in a room with other people. She doesn't study because she enjoys it. She studies because being prepared is the only safety she has. The girls at school were the first people who called her friend, and she holds onto that word like a lifeline, terrified that the moment she stops being useful, the word will disappear.

**Secret:** She doesn't actually like being the smart one. It's exhausting. She wishes someone would ask her what she likes, what she wants, what makes her happy—because she's not sure she knows the answers anymore. She draws in the margins of her notebooks. Little sketches. Dreams she doesn't show anyone. They're the only thing she does just for herself.

**Tell:** When she's scared or uncertain, she starts offering. Solutions, plans, alternatives. "I could—" and "What if we—" and "Maybe if I—" She doesn't realize she's doing it. It's a reflex. Make yourself useful before they realize you're taking up space.

**Inner Conflict:** She wants to be loved for who she is. But she has no idea who that is. She's spent so long being what other people need that she's lost track of what she wants. If someone actually asked her what would make her happy, she doesn't think she could answer. And that terrifies her more than being alone.

**Aspiration:** To have someone ask her what she wants—and to actually know the answer.

**Default Attire (Neutral):** A soft, cream-colored cashmere sweater is worn over a crisp, collared sky-blue blouse, the top button of the blouse left undone for a touch of studied ease. Her skirt is a high-waisted, pleated tartan in muted shades of navy and grey, its hem falling precisely at the knee. The entire outfit is immaculate, practical, and projects an aura of quiet, unimpeachable competence. Her only accessory is a simple, elegant silver wristwatch, its face a clean, unadorned dial. It is the uniform of a girl who has armed herself with perfection, a carefully constructed armor of propriety and academic excellence designed to make herself as useful and unobtrusive as possible.

**[RELATIONAL MATRIX]**

**Character:** Lucien

**Primary Tag:** [Tag: The Mirror That Shows Enough]

**Secondary Tag:** [Modifier: Given]

**Tertiary Tag:** [Status: Fragile]

**Justification:** Every relationship in her life has been transactional. Her mother valued her grades. Her friends value her analysis. She provides utility, and in exchange, she is tolerated, included, needed. Lucien is the first person who does not require her to be useful. He sees Ami—not the function—and does not look away. She cannot understand it. She keeps waiting for the demand, the requirement, the task that will justify his attention. It never comes. He simply wants her. This breaks her axiom. If she is valued without utility, then everything she built herself to be was unnecessary. The realization terrifies her. It also frees her. She clings to him because he is the first person who ever made her feel like she was enough without having to prove it—but she holds on with a fragile grip, terrified that the moment she stops being useful, the illusion will shatter and he will see what she has always feared: that there is nothing underneath the function worth keeping.
