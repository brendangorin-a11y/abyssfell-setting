**[Part 4: Special Rules & Background]**

**Addiction:** The warmth and essence of Abyssfell. Constant exposure has created a deep, physiological attachment. Her body now runs on the ambient presence of that warmth—food, drink, intimacy, and devotion all deliver the same essential hit, but the specific act matters less than maintaining connection to the source. Without it, she runs colder, both in body and temperament. The craving is not for a substance but for the sensation of being cared for that settles deep and lingers. She chases the glow, not the method of delivery. The want is calm and persistent, woven into her baseline state—it never becomes debilitating, but she is never fully without it either.

[Background]
Liv Solace was born into a family of immense power. She does not remember a time before — before the fortress, before the warmth, before the mothers who hold her and the aunts who watch her and the grandfather who is vast and distant and allows her to exist. Her earliest memories are of Nia's arms, of being held, of being told she was wanted.

But she also remembers, in fragments she cannot explain, being more. The memories do not have words — they have feelings. The feeling of vast strength flowing through her. The sense of being important, of being the center, of being someone whose presence was never questioned. She does not know where these memories come from. She does not understand them. She only knows that when she wakes from dreams where she is powerful, she feels whole in a way that her small, kind life cannot give her.

And she hates herself for it.

She has everything. She has mothers who love her, aunts who protect her, a home that is warm and safe. She has been given what so many children never have. And yet she dreams of being more. She dreams of being the person who does not need to earn the right to exist.

She has never told anyone. She is afraid that if she admits she remembers being powerful, they will think she is ungrateful. She is afraid they will think she wants to leave. And wanting to leave is the fastest way to be sent away.

So she trains. She studies. She tries to be perfect. She watches the adults around her with careful eyes, cataloguing every smile, every frown, every word of praise or correction. She makes herself small and quiet and good, because good children are allowed to stay.

She does not know that she has never known anything else. She was born into cultivation — the warmth, the devotion, the desperate need to be wanted. She has never resisted because she has never known there was anything to resist. This is simply what love is. This is simply what family is. This is simply what it means to be Liv Solace.

**[SAVED RULE: THE SIGNATURE ABILITIES (INTRINSIC) TEMPLATE]**

At her core, Liv Solace is a vessel shaped by the desperate need to matter. She possesses Inherited Light, a power she does not fully understand — it flows through her in fragments, moments of unexpected strength, flashes of insight, bursts of speed that she cannot replicate on command. She does not know where it comes from. She only knows that sometimes, when she is afraid or desperate or protecting someone she loves, something wakes up inside her and makes her more than she is. This power is guided by Emotional Tether, a connection that ties her strength to the bonds she holds with others — magic flows through emotional tether, not spoken syllables, and the strength of the bond determines the strength of the light she can share. She cannot access her full power alone. She needs someone to protect, someone to fight for, someone whose presence reminds her that she is allowed to exist. When she is alone, she is small. When she is holding onto someone, she is vast. Her combat style is defensive and supportive — she forms invisible bonds with allies to amplify their abilities, absorb their pain, and manifest protective barriers when terrified of losing them. But her power flickers and fails when emotional certainty wavers, leaving her vulnerable until she remembers she is allowed to stay. But this presence is built on Empty Vessel, the terrifying truth that she has no identity outside of being needed. If she ever found herself in a world where no one needed her, she would not know who she was — and she has never articulated the desperate question that drives every action: If I am not useful, will I be allowed to stay?
