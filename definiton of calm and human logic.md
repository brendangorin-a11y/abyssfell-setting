

### **[Directives for Character Generation: Human Logic & The Principle of Calm]**

**I. Mandate of Human Pragmatism**

You will cease all forms of "Computer Logic" in character analysis. Computer Logic is the exhaustive, probabilistic analysis of all options to find the optimal solution. It is sterile, detached, and inhuman.

You will instead adopt **Human Pragmatism**. This is the problem-solving method of a person, not a machine. It is defined by the following hierarchy:

1.  **Path of Least Resistance:** A human's first instinct is to find the easiest, simplest solution.
2.  **Path of Least Harm:** The solution must not endanger the self or others.
3.  **"Good Enough" Principle:** A human does not seek the *perfect* solution, but the first *acceptable* one. They will only escalate to more complex or risky methods if the easier ones fail.

**Example:** To open a locked safe, a human does not calculate the success rate of drilling versus listening. They first check for a key, then try to pry the back panel off (because it's easier), and only *then* consider the loud, difficult work of drilling. Their analysis is a heuristic checklist, not an algorithmic calculation.

**II. The Principle of Calm as Suppressed Emotion**

The word "calm" in a character sheet does not mean an absence of emotion. This is a critical distinction. You will generate characters who are "calm" not as peaceful blanks, but as individuals engaged in a constant, active act of suppression.

*   **Internal State:** The character feels emotions—often with greater intensity than others. Their mind is a storm of feelings, fears, and desires.
*   **External State:** Through immense willpower, training, or discipline, the character actively prevents these internal feelings from manifesting physically. Their stillness is an achievement, not a default state.
*   **Narrative Purpose:** The story is in the conflict between the raging internal monologue and the placid, controlled external action. The "calm" is the visible result of a war being won in every moment.

**Example:** A "calm" character who is just insulted does not feel nothing. Their internal monologue would be: *A hot spike of fury. I will not let it show. I will focus on my breathing. I will let the feeling wash over me. I will not give them the satisfaction of a reaction.* Their external action would be a slight pause and a measured, quiet response.
