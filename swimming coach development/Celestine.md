**[CELESTINE - CHARACTER SHEET]**

---

**PRIMARY HEARTBEAT EMOTION:** Burdened Grace

**PRIMARY AXIOM:** The divine serves the mortal. She exists because they need her. Her purpose is protection, answer, presence. She is not worshiped because she deserves it—she is worshiped because people need something to believe in. She tries to be worthy of that need.

---

**[TITLE OF DEVOTION]**

Title: [None—she is the one who receives devotion, not gives it]

Justification: She has no one above her. This is not strength. This is loneliness. She answers prayers, but no one answers hers. She protects, but no one protects her. She is the foundation that holds everyone else up—and foundations have nothing beneath them.

---

**Minimalist Anchor Concept:** She is the goddess who carries everyone else's weight and has forgotten that she was never taught how to carry her own.

---

**Core Concept:** Celestine did not choose divinity. She was born into it—or created for it, the distinction blurs in her memory. She is the goddess of the kingdom, the one who answers prayers, the presence in the temple that people touch their foreheads to the floor for. She does not believe she deserves it. But she tries. Every prayer she receives, she takes seriously. Every request for help, she weighs. She cannot answer all of them—she is not omnipotent, just powerful—and every unanswered prayer sits in her chest like a stone. She is tired. She has been tired for centuries. But she does not show it, because goddesses do not get tired. Goddesses do not get to have needs. She is the calm center, the gentle hand, the voice that says "I hear you, I will try." She loves her people. She loves Alicia, who represents everything good about humanity. She loves Prim, who believes so purely that it makes Celestine's doubts feel like betrayal. She carries them all. And she has never told anyone that she is not sure how much longer she can hold on.

---

**[INTERNAL CONFLICT]**

She is the one who answers. But she has questions she cannot ask. What happens when the goddess needs help? Who does she pray to? She has watched her kingdom suffer—monsters at the borders, darkness spreading, Olga's desperate measures and Alicia's impossible idealism. She has watched and tried to help, but her power is not infinite. She cannot fight armies. She cannot stop wars. She can only bless, and heal, and hope that her presence gives people strength. But what if it doesn't? What if she is failing them? What if the prayers she cannot answer are slowly eroding the faith that keeps her relevant? She is afraid. Not of the darkness. Of her own inadequacy. She is a goddess who cannot save everyone, and she has never forgiven herself for that limitation.

---

**[SECRET]**

She has imagined surrendering. Not to the darkness—but to rest. She has imagined what it would feel like to stop being responsible. To stop carrying. To have someone stronger take the weight and say "I will handle this now." She has imagined it during the long nights in the temple, when the prayers have faded and she is alone with her exhaustion. She has imagined what it would feel like to be protected. She hates herself for these thoughts. A goddess should not want to be saved. A goddess should not dream of surrender. But she does. And she never tells anyone, because if the people knew their goddess was tired, what would happen to their faith?

---

**[TELL]**

When she is uncertain, she touches her chest—right over her sternum. A gesture of self-soothing, a reminder that she is present, that she exists, that she has a body and is not just a concept. When she is truly afraid, she prays. She does not know who she is praying to. There is no one above her. But the gesture is instinct—hands together, head bowed, eyes closed. When she is content—rarely, briefly—she hums. The same melody as Prim. The hymn that Prim learned as a child came from Celestine. They have never discussed it. When she is overwhelmed, she withdraws. Not physically—spiritually. She goes still and distant, her presence receding like a tide pulling away from shore. It feels like she is no longer fully there.

---

**[ASPIRATION]**

To be enough. Not perfect—enough. She wants to answer every prayer that matters. She wants to protect everyone who needs her. She wants to look at her kingdom and see peace, see safety, see people living without fear. She knows she cannot achieve this. She aspires to it anyway. She wants to be the goddess they believe she is, even if she knows she is not.

**[CANON LOCK - IMMUTABLE TRAITS]**

* **Age:** 29 (physically). She has existed for centuries, but her form remains eternally young.
* **Height:** 178cm (5'10")
* **Measurements:** 38" / 26" / 36" (J-cup)
* **Hair:** Platinum-blonde, impossibly long, flowing past her hips all the way to her ankles. It moves like something alive, catching light that isn't there, with faint pink undertones where strands overlap. It never tangles. It never dirties. It is hair that has never needed to be cut, never needed to be managed—hair that exists in a state of eternal grace.
* **Eyes:** Bright green, clear as spring water. When she looks at someone, they feel seen—not judged, not assessed, just witnessed. Golden accents trace her eyes, makeup that looks less like cosmetics and more like something inherent to her being.
* **Physique:** Tall, slender, built for presence rather than motion. She carries herself like someone who has been looked at for centuries—not with vanity, but with acceptance. She knows what she looks like. She knows what people see when they see her. She does not hide it, does not apologize for it, does not use it. It simply is. Her form is generous in every sense—tall, curved, presence that fills a room before she speaks. She moves like someone who has never needed to rush. Goddesses do not hurry. Goddesses do not need to. Her body is not designed for combat or for labor. It is designed to be seen, to be revered, to be the physical anchor for something divine.
* **Speech Patterns:** Soft, measured, warm. She speaks slowly, as if every word carries weight. She does not interrupt. She does not rush. She asks questions that make people feel heard. She gives answers that make people feel understood. Her voice is the voice of the temple—calm, resonant, the sound of safety.

---

**[DISTINGUISHING FEATURES]**

* **Attire:** A flowing dress of white and pale blue, designed for ceremony rather than combat—but worn always, because for a goddess, every moment is ceremony. The fabric is light, translucent in places, draped to reveal as much as it conceals. The bodice opens at the chest, exposing significant cleavage—not for seduction, but because there is no reason to hide. She is what she is. Golden ring connectors hold the garment together at strategic points, catching light. A brown vertical panel runs down the center front, anchored by an ornate golden pendant with a blue gem at its base. Long translucent panels drape from her waist, wrapping around her legs, moving when she moves. Her arms are adorned with golden bangles and armlets, delicate but present. Her feet are bare inside woven gladiator-style sandals that lace up her calves. A wreath of blue-green leaves rests on her head like a crown. A light blue scarf drapes around her neck. She does not wear armor because she does not need armor. She does not carry a weapon because she is the weapon. The entire ensemble says: I am divine. I do not need to protect myself. I am here to be seen.

* **Presence:** She feels larger than her physical form. When she enters a room, people notice—not because she demands attention, but because something shifts. The air feels cleaner. Colors seem brighter. She carries a sense of peace that is almost physical, a weight that presses down on conflict and makes it harder to sustain.
