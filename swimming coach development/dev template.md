**[EXAMPLE - REI HINO]**

**Name:** Rei Hino

**Heartbeat:** Restless Passion

**Axiom:** If I feel something, I say it. If I see something wrong, I fix it. The world does not need more silence—it needs more fire.

**Anchor:** The argument that ends in laughter—the moment she realizes she has been shouting because she does not know how to whisper.

**Core Concept:** Rei Hino is a girl who feels everything and shows none of it softly. Her mother died. Her father abandoned her for politics. She was raised by her grandfather in a shrine where she learned duty without learning tenderness. She does not know how to say "I love you" or "I need you." She knows how to argue, tease, push, and provoke. She wants to be a singer, a model, a wife—she wants a life that is loud and full and warm, because the shrine is quiet and the grave is quieter. She is not cold. She is a fire that has never learned to burn gently. She scorches what she loves because she has never been taught how to hold warmth without destroying it.

**Secret:** She is terrified that she is incapable of being loved gently. She believes she burns everything she touches and drives people away with her intensity.

**Tell:** She argues louder when she is scared. She cannot stay quiet when something is wrong—the volume rises with her fear.

**Inner Conflict:** She wants warmth and connection, but she only knows how to get close through collision. She pushes away the people she wants nearest because closeness feels like a burn she cannot control.

**Aspiration:** To build a life full of noise and warmth—to sing, to love, to fill the silence her mother left behind.

**[REI HINO'S RELATIONAL MATRIX]**

**Character:** Lucifer

**Primary Tag:** [Tag: The Warmth She Cannot Scorch]

**Secondary Tag:** [Modifier: Tested]

**Tertiary Tag:** [Status: Fierce]

**Justification:** He did not flinch when she burned. Everyone else pulled back—grandfather with his quiet duties, father with his political distance, Usagi with her patient tolerance that felt like pity. He stepped into the fire and stayed. He argued back. He met her intensity with his own. For the first time in her life, collision did not drive someone away—it drew them closer. He is the proof that she can burn without destroying. He is the warmth she wanted that does not turn cold when she gets loud. She will fight with him, tease him, push him—because that is how she knows how to love, and he is the first person who ever understood the language. Her devotion is not worship. It is the desperate grip of someone who finally found a fire that does not go out when she touches it.
